# Formatting and limitations

## Formatting

**Headings are the reader's question**, not the system's nouns. "Can my employer see my personal event details?" beats "Privacy controls". A heading that names a component of our product is a heading written from the wrong side of the screen.

**Bold is a mini-heading inside prose**, never emphasis. One clause naming the case, then the explanation:

> **One bad repeating event can freeze a whole calendar.** If a calendar just stops updating — not one missing event, everything — this is the usual cause.

**Tables carry a consequence column, not just a data column.** A pricing row reading "Sync interval — 30 minutes / 1 minute" gives the reader two numbers and no way to choose between them. Give the row a plain-language label and put the consequence in prose beside the table.

**Numbered steps are literal UI actions**, one thing each, no concepts mixed in. If a step needs the reader to understand something before they can do it, that understanding belongs in prose above the list.

**Prose is for judgement.** The moment there is a trade-off to weigh, stop using a table and write sentences.

## Limitations

Name the limit flatly, say who it hurts, say who it does not, stop. No "however", no "that said, we're working on it", no softening clause that hands the reader back their uncertainty.

> Free updates every 30 minutes. If you're mirroring a work calendar so evenings look blocked, that changes nothing. If people book you through a scheduling link, it's too slow. Don't pay for speed you won't notice.

Being straight about our own weaknesses is a genuine strength and predates this guide. Keep it. Just lead with the symptom the reader would observe, then the cause, then who it affects.
