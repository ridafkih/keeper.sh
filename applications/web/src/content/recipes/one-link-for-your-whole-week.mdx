**Plan: Free.** Uses 1 of 1 iCal feed and no connections.

You do not want your calendars merged into each other — you want one place that shows everything. A feed does that without writing a single event anywhere.

## What you build

One [iCal feed](/docs/ical-feeds) containing every calendar you have connected, subscribed to from the app you actually check.

## Steps

1. Connect the accounts whose calendars you want to see. Free allows two providers.
2. Open **iCal Feeds** in the dashboard and open your feed.
3. Under **Calendars**, tick every calendar that should appear.
4. Read the disclosure line under the feed link. On Free it will say the link shares event times only, and every event will read "Busy".
5. Copy the link and subscribe to it in your calendar app — usually "Add calendar" and then "From URL" or "Subscribe".

## Why this fits the Free plan so well

Connections are the scarce resource on Free; feeds are not counted against them. If a calendar only needs to be *seen* somewhere, a feed does the job at no cost to your three connections. [Why you cannot connect another calendar](/guides/cannot-connect-another-calendar) makes the same point from the other direction.

## What it will not do

- **It is read-only.** You cannot create events in a subscribed feed. Keep creating them in the real calendars.
- **It refreshes on the app's schedule.** Subscribed calendars are pulled by the app, and some apps are hours apart. If a stale view is the problem, read [why the calendar you subscribed to is out of date](/guides/subscribed-link-out-of-date).
- **On Free it shows times only.** If you want your own titles back in your own feed, that is a Pro feed setting.
