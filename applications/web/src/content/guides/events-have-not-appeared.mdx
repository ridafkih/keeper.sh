A new event reaches your other calendars within 30 minutes on Free and within a minute on Pro. If it has been longer than that, work down this list — the first two causes account for most missing events.

## Has enough time passed?

Keeper.sh checks your calendars every minute on both plans, but delivery is not instant. On Free, two minutes of nothing is normal — wait out the half hour before treating it as a fault.

Calendar apps add their own delay on top. Your app decides when to re-read a subscribed link, and some wait hours — see [a subscribed calendar is showing out-of-date events](/guides/subscribed-calendar-out-of-date).

## Are events being sent from this calendar to that one?

Events only travel in the direction you set up. A connection from A to B copies events created in A into B, and does nothing for events created in B.

Open the calendar the event was created in and check **Send Events to Calendars**. If the other calendar is not ticked there, no connection runs in that direction. [Sending events to another calendar](/docs/sending-events-to-another-calendar) covers adding the second direction.

## Is the event inside the date range?

Keeper.sh copies events from one month back to two years ahead by default. An event outside that range is not copied, which usually explains a missing event far in the future or one dragged well into the past.

The ranges are set per calendar under **Sync Window**, up to two years in each direction. Changing them needs Pro. [How far events are copied](/docs/how-far-events-are-copied) has the detail.

## Is the event excluded?

Exclusions leave whole categories out: all-day events, and on Google, focus time and out-of-office blocks. They are off until someone turns them on. If the missing event is a birthday, a holiday or a focus block, check **Exclusions** on the calendar it was created in.

## Did you hit a limit?

If you set the connection up recently and it did not take, you may have hit the Free plan's limit of 3 connections. [Adding another calendar is blocked by a plan limit](/guides/blocked-by-a-plan-limit) covers what that looks like.

## Is the account still connected?

Google or Outlook can revoke access after a password change, a revoked app-specific password, or an administrator policy. If an account has stopped working, reconnect it from the dashboard and the waiting changes flow again.
