An option is greyed out, or the dashboard tells you a limit has been reached. Keeper.sh counts three separate things, and knowing which one you have run into changes what you can do about it.

## Which limit did you hit?

- **Linked accounts** — one connected provider each. Free allows 2.
- **Connections** — one source calendar sending to one destination calendar. Free allows 3.
- **iCal feeds** — one subscribable link each. Free allows 1.

Pro is unlimited on all three. [Plans and limits](/docs/plans-and-limits) sets them out in a table.

## Accounts count providers, not calendars

This is the one that surprises people. Connecting a Google account brings in every calendar in it, and that is still one account. Two Google accounts are two accounts, even if you only care about one calendar in each.

So if the account limit is what stopped you, ask whether the calendar you want is already inside a provider you have connected. It often is, sitting in the calendar list of an account you added weeks ago.

## Connections are counted per pair of calendars

Sending one calendar to two destinations is two connections. A two-way pair between two calendars is also two. That is how three goes quickly: one two-way pair plus a single extra direction fills a Free plan exactly.

If you are over, consider whether every direction earns its place. Copying your work calendar into your personal one so you can see your day is one connection; copying it back is a second, and only worth it if you actually create work events in the personal calendar.

## A feed can replace several connections

If a destination calendar exists only to be *looked at* — a phone that should show everything, a shared screen, a second app — a feed does that with no connections at all. One feed can carry every calendar you have, and it writes nothing into anyone's calendar. That is the cheapest way to see everything in one place on Free, and it is what the [one link with your whole week](/recipes/one-link-for-your-whole-week) recipe builds.

## If you are already on Pro

Pro has no counts to hit, so a refusal there is something else: an account whose access was revoked at the provider, or a calendar that cannot receive events. A calendar subscribed from a link is read-only and can never be a destination — [linked accounts and calendars](/docs/accounts-and-calendars) covers which calendars can do what.
