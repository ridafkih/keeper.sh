The sync window is the span of time a calendar accepts copies for. It sits on the calendar **receiving** events, under **Sync Window**, and it applies to everything copied into that calendar.

## Two ranges

**Sync Historic Events** sets how far into the past copies reach, and defaults to one month. **Sync Future Events** sets how far ahead, and defaults to two years. Each takes one of six spans: 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 12 months, or 2 years. An event outside the window is not copied, however recently it was created.

Choosing a span is a Pro feature. On Free the defaults apply and the dashboard says as much where the setting would be.

## Narrowing a window removes events

This is the part worth reading twice. Narrowing a range does not only stop new copies being made outside it — it also removes copies already sitting in that calendar outside the new range. That is deliberate: the window describes what the calendar should contain, not only what it should accept next.

Widening a range works the other way. Events inside the newly covered span are copied in on the next sync rather than immediately, so give it a cycle before concluding something is wrong. [How quickly a change lands](/docs/connections) covers the timings.

If events you expected have gone missing, [why events vanished from the calendar receiving them](/guides/events-vanished-from-destination) starts from that symptom.

## Choosing a range

A narrow future range keeps a shared or work calendar tidy and stops next year's provisional plans leaking into it. A wide historic range is useful when the destination calendar is the one you search through for what you did and when.

Every event inside the window is a real event in the destination calendar, so a wide window on a busy source is a lot of events. If a destination calendar is only there to be read, an [iCal feed](/docs/ical-feeds) may fit better than a connection: a feed is one link rather than thousands of copies.
