A linked account is one calendar service you have signed into: Google, Outlook, iCloud, Fastmail, a CalDAV server, or a calendar link you paste in. Every calendar inside it gets its own page in Keeper.sh, and that page is where all the calendar's settings live.

One sign-in is one account, however many calendars sit inside it. Plan limits count accounts and connections, never calendars, so a Google account with eight calendars still uses one slot. The counts are on [plans and limits](/docs/plans-and-limits).

## What each calendar service asks you for

You connect from the dashboard's connect screen. What you are asked for depends on the calendar:

- **Google, Outlook and Microsoft** — sign in on their own permission screen and grant calendar access. Keeper.sh never sees your password.
- **iCloud and Fastmail** — an app-specific password, made in their settings. It is a stand-in password that only unlocks your calendar, so your real one stays with Apple or Fastmail.
- **Anything else** — a CalDAV server address with sign-in details, or a calendar link you paste in.

Connecting copies nothing by itself. Events only move once you [send them to another calendar](/docs/sending-events-to-another-calendar).

## What each calendar can do

A calendar's page lists what Keeper.sh can do with it under **Calendar Information**, and that decides which settings appear:

- A calendar Keeper.sh can **read** can send its events out. Its page carries **Send Events to Calendars**, [what a copied event shows](/docs/what-a-copied-event-shows) and **Exclusions**.
- A calendar Keeper.sh can **write to** can receive events. Its page carries [how far back and ahead events are copied](/docs/how-far-events-are-copied).

A calendar subscribed from a link is read-only, so it can send events but never receive them. A calendar you own can usually do both.

## Renaming a calendar

**Calendar Name** renames a calendar inside Keeper.sh only. The name at Google or Outlook does not change, and nobody you share a link with sees it. Rename when two services both call a calendar "Calendar" and you have to tell them apart in a list.

## Removing an account

Disconnecting an account stops Keeper.sh reading or writing it and frees the slot on your plan. Events already copied into other calendars stay there — delete them in that calendar if you want them gone.
