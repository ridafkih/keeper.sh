You have two calendars and you keep getting double-booked. A calendar sync tool copies your events from one to the other. Both then show you as busy at the same times.

Nine tools do that job. All nine are in the table below, including Keeper, which we build, and including the ones that beat it.

Three other products get recommended for this and do something else entirely. Calendly, Zapier and IFTTT have their own section near the end.

## How we compared these

We did not race these tools against each other. Every number about another tool is what that vendor publishes, and each one is linked.

Where a vendor publishes no figure, the table says **Not published**. That is a real answer, and it comes up a lot.

Keeper's numbers work differently. Keeper is [open source](https://github.com/ridafkih/keeper.sh), so you can read the code that produces them.

## Which one should you get?

### Best free plan: Keeper

Keeper's free plan covers two calendar accounts and three connections between them. Your events reach the other calendar every 30 minutes.

[SyncDate's free plan](https://syncdate.app/pricing) is much faster but covers one connection. If speed matters more to you than count, take that one instead.

### Best for speed: SyncDate

[SyncDate publishes about four seconds](https://syncdate.app/features) for Google and Outlook. Google and Microsoft tell it the moment something changes, and it checks again every 15 minutes as a backstop.

Keeper is slower, and that is worth saying plainly. Your events reach the other calendar every minute on Pro, and every 30 minutes on the free plan.

### Best for privacy: OGCS

[OGCS](https://www.outlookgooglecalendarsync.com/) runs on your own Windows PC. Your calendar data travels straight between Outlook and Google, never through anyone else's server.

If you need a signed data processing agreement and hosting inside the EU, [Kalender Sync publishes both](https://kalender-sync.de/en/it-admin/). Keeper offers neither today.

### Best for self-hosting: Keeper

Keeper is the only tool here you can run on your own server. It is AGPL-3.0, and every Pro feature is included when you self-host.

OGCS is open source too, but it is a Windows program rather than a server. There is a full section on running Keeper yourself further down.

### Best for iCloud: CalendarBridge

[CalendarBridge treats iCloud as a first-class calendar](https://help.calendarbridge.com/faq/how-often-do-calendars-sync/) and tells you what to expect: every five to ten minutes. It also ships apps for Windows, iPhone and Android.

Keeper, SyncDate, OneCal, Morgen, Kalender Sync and SyncThemCalendars all work with iCloud as well. [Reclaim.ai does not](https://help.reclaim.ai/en/articles/3600762-calendar-sync-overview-keep-multiple-schedules-in-sync).

### Best for teams: CalendarBridge

An admin connects everyone's calendars from one place. They can [set which domains staff are allowed to sync with](https://help.calendarbridge.com/faq/do-i-need-a-group-plan/), and pay one bill for the whole group.

[Kalender Sync](https://kalender-sync.de/en/pricing/) is the better answer for a European team. It publishes single sign-on, org-wide rules and instant offboarding when someone leaves.

Keeper has no team features at all. One person, one account, one bill.

## The nine tools

### Keeper

- **Best for** — people who want to run it themselves, or who want busy blocks without leaking detail
- **Price** — free, or $5/month for Pro. Free covers 2 calendar accounts and 3 connections
- **How fast changes show up** — every calendar is read every minute on every plan. Your events reach the other calendar every 30 minutes on free, every minute on Pro
- **Which way events travel** — one direction per connection. Both ways is two connections
- **Calendars it works with** — Google, Outlook, iCloud, Fastmail and CalDAV can each read and write. ICS links are read-only
- **What it does best** — it is open source, and self-hosting gives everyone on your server the Pro features
- **Where it lets you down** — no mobile app, no team accounts, no signed data processing agreement, and it is slower than several tools here

Keeper hides event titles, descriptions and locations by default, and shows the calendar's name instead. Changing what shows through is a Pro feature. There is also a REST API and an MCP server if you want your own tools or an agent to manage your calendars.

### SyncDate

- **Best for** — anyone who needs changes to land in seconds, and European buyers who need paperwork
- **Price** — [plans start at $8.99/month](https://syncdate.app/pricing). Free covers 2 calendars, 2 accounts and 1 sync
- **How fast changes show up** — [about four seconds](https://syncdate.app/features) on Google and Outlook, with a 15-minute backup check
- **Which way events travel** — one or both, set per sync
- **Calendars it works with** — Google, Outlook and CalDAV servers including iCloud, Fastmail and Nextcloud. ICS links are read-only
- **What it does best** — the fastest published figure of any tool here, by a wide margin
- **Where it lets you down** — [no self-hosting](https://syncdate.app/compare/syncdate-vs-keeper), and no mobile app

SyncDate [runs in Hetzner data centres in Germany and Finland](https://syncdate.app/security), operates as a Romanian company, and offers a [self-serve data processing agreement](https://syncdate.app/dpa). It also states plainly that it is not itself SOC 2 certified. If a compliance team is asking you questions, this is the strongest set of answers on the list.

### OneCal

- **Best for** — a small team that wants single sign-on without paying an enterprise price
- **Price** — [$5, $10 or $25 per user per month](https://www.onecal.io/pricing), by calendar count. No free plan, 14-day trial
- **How fast changes show up** — ["real-time"](https://docs.onecal.io/docs/calendar-sync/calendar-sync-intro), with [iCloud updates taking up to 10 minutes](https://www.onecal.io/features/apple-calendar-sync)
- **Which way events travel** — one or many directions between two or more calendars
- **Calendars it works with** — [Google, Outlook and iCloud](https://www.onecal.io/)
- **What it does best** — ["We don't store any calendar event data"](https://www.onecal.io/), and iPhone and Android apps
- **Where it lets you down** — no CalDAV, no Fastmail, no free plan, and [SOC 2 is still an audit in progress](https://security.onecal.io/)

Single sign-on, SCIM and admin-managed accounts are on every plan, including the $5 one. That is unusual, and it is the reason to pick OneCal over a bigger name.

### CalendarBridge

- **Best for** — teams, and anyone whose main calendar is iCloud
- **Price** — [$4, $8 or $32 per month billed yearly](https://calendarbridge.com/pricing/). No free plan; trial length is not published
- **How fast changes show up** — [within a minute on Google and Outlook, every 5 to 10 minutes on iCloud and ICS](https://help.calendarbridge.com/faq/how-often-do-calendars-sync/)
- **Which way events travel** — [one direction per connection](https://help.calendarbridge.com/user-docs/about-syncing/); stack two for both ways
- **Calendars it works with** — [Google, Microsoft 365, Outlook, Exchange, iCloud and ICS](https://calendarbridge.com/real-time-calendar-syncing/)
- **What it does best** — an admin sets up everyone's calendars, and no staff member has to do anything
- **Where it lets you down** — no self-hosting, and [SOC 2 Type 2 is in progress rather than done](https://trust.calendarbridge.com/)

CalendarBridge is the only tool here that names its mechanism for every kind of calendar it supports. It says which ones get told about changes and which ones get checked on a timer, and how often. That honesty is why it wins two categories above.

### Reclaim.ai

- **Best for** — people who want an AI assistant to arrange their week, with calendar sync as a side effect
- **Price** — [free, then $10, $15 or $22 per seat per month](https://reclaim.ai/pricing). Free covers 1 calendar sync
- **How fast changes show up** — ["in real time"](https://reclaim.ai/features/calendar-sync) with no figure published
- **Which way events travel** — [one direction per rule](https://help.reclaim.ai/en/articles/3654852-how-to-set-up-a-calendar-sync-in-reclaim); set up two rules for both ways
- **Calendars it works with** — [Google and Outlook only](https://help.reclaim.ai/en/articles/3600762-calendar-sync-overview-keep-multiple-schedules-in-sync)
- **What it does best** — [a completed SOC 2 Type II](https://reclaim.ai/security), which no other tool here publishes
- **Where it lets you down** — no iCloud, no CalDAV, no mobile app, and unlimited syncing needs the $15 seat

Reclaim is not really a sync tool, and it does not claim to be. It calls itself an AI calendar, and syncing is one feature among habits, tasks and scheduling links. If you only want two calendars to agree, you are buying a lot of product you will not use.

### SyncThemCalendars

- **Best for** — someone who lives in Google Workspace and wants this set up in a couple of minutes
- **Price** — [$3.99, $7.99 or $17.99 per month](https://syncthemcalendars.com/pricing), by sync count. No free plan, 14-day trial
- **How fast changes show up** — ["in real time"](https://syncthemcalendars.com) with no figure published
- **Which way events travel** — [one or both](https://syncthemcalendars.com), your choice
- **Calendars it works with** — [Google, Outlook and iCloud](https://syncthemcalendars.com/pricing)
- **What it does best** — the cheapest paid entry price here, and a genuinely short setup
- **Where it lets you down** — no free plan, no CalDAV, no team plan, and nothing published about encryption or a data processing agreement

It installs as a Google Workspace add-on rather than a separate site. That is convenient if Google is where you already are, and awkward if it is not.

### Kalender Sync

- **Best for** — anyone in Europe who has to answer to a privacy officer
- **Price** — [free, then €4 or €10 per month](https://kalender-sync.de/en/pricing/). Free covers 2 accounts and 1 sharing
- **How fast changes show up** — [seconds to a few minutes](https://kalender-sync.de/en/integrations/) on Google and Outlook for paid plans, 30 minutes on free, every 5 minutes for iCloud
- **Which way events travel** — [one or both](https://kalender-sync.de/en/), your choice
- **Calendars it works with** — Google, Outlook, iCloud, Infomaniak, GMX, WEB.DE, mailbox.org and read-only ICS links
- **What it does best** — [databases in Falkenstein, Germany, a signed GDPR agreement, and named subprocessors](https://kalender-sync.de/en/it-admin/)
- **Where it lets you down** — no self-hosting, no mobile app, and speed is a paid feature

Kalender Sync writes anonymised busy blocks by default and does not carry event content or attendee lists unless you turn that on. Fastmail, Proton and Nextcloud are listed as coming, not shipped. For a German or Austrian company, this is the tool that will clear review fastest.

### Morgen

- **Best for** — people who want one calendar app on every device, with copying between calendars as a bonus
- **Price** — [$15 per month billed yearly, or $30 monthly](https://www.morgen.so/pricing). No free plan, 14-day trial
- **How fast changes show up** — the calendar-to-calendar workflow [runs once a day at 6pm and covers the next 14 days](https://www.morgen.so/guides/how-to-use-morgens-calendar-propagation-workflow)
- **Which way events travel** — one direction; two-way copying is not published
- **Calendars it works with** — [Google, Outlook, iCloud, Fastmail, many CalDAV servers and ICS feeds](https://www.morgen.so/integrations)
- **What it does best** — real apps on Windows, Mac, Linux, iPhone and Android, which nothing else here matches
- **Where it lets you down** — the daily schedule and 14-day horizon make it the slowest and narrowest copier on this list

Morgen is a calendar app first. [Its data lives in Switzerland or the EU](https://www.morgen.so/privacy) and it publishes a data processing agreement. Buy it for the app, not for the syncing.

### OGCS

- **Best for** — a Windows user with Outlook installed who wants this to cost nothing and touch no server
- **Price** — free, open source under MPL-2.0. Donations optional
- **How fast changes show up** — [every 15 minutes at the fastest](https://www.outlookgooglecalendarsync.com/guide/syncoptions-when.html). It can also spot changes you make in Outlook within 2 minutes, but then the scheduled run drops to every 120 minutes
- **Which way events travel** — [one or both](https://github.com/phw198/OutlookGoogleCalendarSync), your choice
- **Calendars it works with** — desktop Outlook and Google Calendar, nothing else
- **What it does best** — your calendar data never reaches a third party, and it publishes more about repeating events and attendees than anything else here
- **Where it lets you down** — Windows only, needs Outlook installed, no iCloud, no CalDAV, and it only runs when your PC is on

OGCS syncs repeating events as a series, copies attendees including whether they are required or optional, and asks before deleting anything. Two caveats worth stating: it reports usage figures to the developer unless you turn that off, and it stores your Google address for its own records. Neither involves your events.

## Tools people recommend for this that do something else

These three come up constantly in answers to "how do I sync two calendars". None of them is a calendar sync tool, and only one of them says so.

### Calendly

Calendly is a booking tool. Its own help pages describe the calendar connection precisely: ["Calendly checks your calendar for busy times and adds new meetings to it"](https://calendly.com/help/connect-your-calendar-to-calendly).

That is reading your busy times and writing the meetings it books. It does not copy your existing events from one calendar to another. The free plan connects one calendar; [paid plans connect six](https://calendly.com/pricing).

### Zapier

Zapier says it outright: ["Zapier does not support two-way syncing at this time"](https://help.zapier.com/hc/en-us/articles/8496232045453-Zap-is-stuck-in-a-loop). The same page warns that loops "will repeat the same data over and over".

Repeating events are a documented problem too. ["Repeated events in Google Calendar aren't supported with the New Event trigger"](https://help.zapier.com/hc/en-us/articles/8495964746381-Common-Problems-with-Google-Calendar), and editing a series fires once per occurrence.

Its free plan [checks for new things every 15 minutes](https://help.zapier.com/hc/en-us/articles/8496244568589-How-Zap-triggers-work), dropping to 2 minutes on Professional and 1 minute on Team.

### IFTTT

IFTTT can create a calendar event. Looking at [its Google Calendar service](https://ifttt.com/google_calendar), the only actions are "Quick add event" and "Create a detailed event".

There is no update action and no delete action. So an event you move or cancel has no published way to change on the other calendar. [The iOS Calendar service](https://ifttt.com/ios_calendar) has one action and the same gap.

## Where calendar sync actually breaks

Most comparison pages stop at price and speed. The things that ruin a synced calendar are elsewhere, and almost nobody publishes how they handle them.

**A repeating event is not one event.** Your Monday standup is a rule, not fifty meetings. Move one week of it, and a tool that treats the series as a single item moves all fifty.

Keeper syncs repeating events as a series, and handles the one-off exceptions inside it.

**Daylight saving breaks naive tools.** A 9am recurring meeting has to stay 9am when the clocks change. A tool that stored it as a fixed moment in time will move it by an hour twice a year.

**Deletions are harder than additions.** If you cancel a meeting and the copy stays behind, you are still blocked. Keeper removes the copy when the event goes, and removes every copy it made if you disconnect a calendar.

**Attendees are the part to be careful about.** Keeper does not copy attendees, meeting links or reminders onto the other calendar, so nobody gets invited twice. It does read attendees, so you can accept an invitation or change your answer from inside Keeper.

If you want the long version of why any of this is hard, we wrote it up in [how calendar sync actually works](/blog/how-calendar-sync-actually-works).

## Running Keeper on your own server

Keeper is open source under AGPL-3.0, and running it yourself is a real option rather than a token gesture. Everyone on your server gets the Pro features, including updates every minute and full control over what shows through.

The honest cost is a server, a domain, and being the person who notices when it stops. You will handle updates and backups yourself. If you want Google or Outlook connected, you also register your own app with each of them.

The [README on GitHub](https://github.com/ridafkih/keeper.sh/blob/main/README.md) has a single Docker Compose file that brings up everything at once. iCloud, Fastmail, CalDAV and ICS links work with no extra setup.

If none of that appeals, [the hosted version](https://www.keeper.sh/register) is the same software with none of the upkeep.

## FAQ

### Which calendar sync tool is fastest?

SyncDate, on its published figure of about four seconds for Google and Outlook. CalendarBridge publishes "within a minute" for the same calendars.

For iCloud, nothing is fast. Apple offers no way to be told about changes, so every tool checks on a timer instead.

### Is there a free calendar sync tool?

Yes, several. OGCS is completely free with no limits, if you use Windows with Outlook installed.

Keeper, SyncDate, Kalender Sync and Reclaim.ai all have permanent free plans. OneCal, CalendarBridge, SyncThemCalendars and Morgen offer trials only.

### Can I sync iCloud with Google Calendar?

Yes. Keeper, SyncDate, OneCal, CalendarBridge, SyncThemCalendars, Kalender Sync and Morgen all support iCloud.

You will need an app-specific password from Apple, which is a separate password that only gives calendar access. Reclaim.ai does not support iCloud at all.

### Does Keeper do two-way sync?

Not in one step. Each connection carries events in one direction, so both ways means setting up two connections.

This is deliberate. It makes it obvious which calendar is the one you edit, and which one is showing a copy.

### Will my colleagues see my private appointments?

Not by default. Keeper replaces the title with the calendar's name and drops the description and location.

Your dentist appointment shows up as a block of busy time with no detail. Letting more through is a Pro feature, and it is off until you turn it on.

### Can I use Zapier to sync two calendars?

Zapier's own documentation advises against it. It states that two-way syncing is not supported and that loops repeat the same data endlessly.

Repeating events are also unsupported on its main Google Calendar trigger. A tool built for calendars will cost you less trouble.

### What happens to the copies if I stop using a tool?

That depends on the tool, and it is worth checking before you start. Keeper removes every event it created when you disconnect a calendar.

SyncDate and OneCal both let you choose whether to keep or remove the copies. OGCS asks before it deletes anything.
